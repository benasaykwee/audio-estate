IWhat the software is

A machine that listens to a recording, says what kind of thing made the sound, and hands the body to the instrument best equipped to raise it again.

CORONER is the sixth member of the audio estate and the first that listens instead of speaking. AUTOPSY shapes, RIGOR squeezes, CASKET seals, SÉANCE dresses a voice, NECROPHONE and PALLBEARER make sound from nothing. CORONER takes sound apart.

You give it an audio file. It measures thirty-three properties of that file — how steadily the pitch is held, whether the partials stretch the way a stiff string's do, how much noise sits under the tone, how the energy arrived and whether anything is still putting energy in. From those measurements it reaches a verdict about what kind of source produced the sound. Then it writes a routing slip: which instrument in the estate should take this, which of its engines, and a starting patch built from the numbers it just measured.

Click the button on that slip and NECROPHONE opens with the patch already loaded. That handoff reuses NECROPHONE's own share-link format, so nothing in NECROPHONE had to change for CORONER to exist. It can also emit a pack with the sample inside it, which is the handoff that actually works — a URL cannot hold audio, and the patch is measured from audio.

Every other program in this estate makes sound. This one takes sound apart, writes down what it found, and says where the body goes. The one-sentence version

What it is not

It is not a transcription tool — it does not write out the notes. It is not a stem separator — it cannot pull a bass line out of a mix. It is not a sound-matching engine — it does not yet reproduce the sound you gave it, only characterise it and suggest a starting point. Each of those is a real thing it could become, and each has a place in the roadmap below. None of them is what it does today.

And it is version 0.1. The classifier has been proved against synthesized bodies whose nature is known by construction, and measured against thirteen real files. It has not been proved against a room, a microphone, or a mix bus — and each of those changes the very measurements it relies on. Sections IV·b and V are the honest accounting of what that means, and they are the two most important sections in this report.

IIThe three layers

The separation is the entire architectural argument.

CORONER is built in three layers that do not reach into each other. This is not tidiness — it is the answer to the question Ben actually asked, which was what has the most room to grow, and to use AI later?

The insight is that the choice was never machine learning instead of signal processing. Both approaches need the same thing first: a layer that turns audio into meaningful numbers. Build that layer well and it becomes the training input for a model later, rather than work thrown away.

CASKET's chooseArrangement(), written by a parallel session the same day, deliberately mirrors this same split — a thin replaceable function reading a features object. The pattern is now the estate's convention for anything that forms an opinion.

The other twenty-six

Pitch confidence, vibrato rate and depth, harmonicity, odd/even balance, partial irregularity, spectral slope, brightness, timbral motion, rolloff, spectral flatness, flux, sub and air energy, tremolo rate and depth, onset density, crest factor, zero-crossing rate, voice estimate, duration, stereo width — each declared in a registry with its range, its unit, and a note saying what it means and where it lies.

Why a registry and not a plain object. A model needs to enumerate its own input vector, know each field's range, and fail loudly when the version it trained on stops matching the one it is handed. The vector's order is pinned by a test, because a reordered vector is read out of the wrong slots and fails silently — which is the worst way to fail.

IVThe build record

Where the faults came from, and what that says about testing audio software.

CORONER went from nothing to 168 checks in a single session. The interesting part is not the count — it is which method found which bug, because the three methods found completely different classes of fault and none of them could have found the others'.

The five that real audio caught

1 · Jitter measured the melody, not the note. 128 checks were green. Then eight real NECROPHONE renders reported pitch jitter of 250 to 435 cents. Nothing was unstable — it was reading the tune. Since near-zero jitter is the strongest synthetic tell, and no piece of music can show near-zero jitter under that definition, the tell could never fire on real material at all. An oscillator pad came back "Blown or Reed".

2 · The harmonic analysis averaged a whole phrase, so partial tracking found nothing and stiffness came back at exactly 0.00 for every file — bells included.

3 · Unison detune imitates string stiffness. A pad made of nothing but oscillators fitted B=3.09e−4 and was routed to the physical engine on the strength of it.

4 · A vibrato smears its own evidence. The same body fitted 0.495 with vibrato against 0.999 held still, and a genuine stiffness measurement was thrown away.

5 · Pitch detection reports a stiff string sharp — 112 Hz for a 110 Hz body. That is real physics, not a coding error, but it poisons every deviation measured against it. Fixed by solving pitch and stiffness together: squaring the stiff-string relation makes it a straight line, and one least-squares fit returns both. Result: 109.95 Hz and B=8.04e−4 against a true 110 and 8.00e−4.

A harness of single held notes cannot see what real audio does. Every synthesized body was one steady note, and the most confident wrong answer in the machine was invisible to all of them. The lesson, now logged for every project in the estate

The three that only sabotage caught

Mutation testing means deliberately breaking the code and confirming a named check fails. Seventeen sabotages were run. Four survived the first pass — each one a place where the code was correct but nothing was guarding it:

A steady tone fragmenting into six notes. Both onset thresholds were relative, and both collapse toward zero on steady material. The absolute floor was missing — which is the exact lesson already quoted in the comment directly above the bug.

A jitter figure that ran backwards. 12.3, then 7.1, then 0.8 cents as real instability rose, because the segmenter was chopping a wobbling note into the moments it happened to be still. A measurement that runs backwards is worse than no measurement, because it will be believed.

A glide fusing into one note, once the rule that a pitch change must persist was added — arriving at the original whole-file bug by a completely different road.

IV · bThe closed loop

Render an engine, feed the audio back in, see where it routes. Two of eight came home — and the strongest tell in the machine was running backwards.

While CORONER was being built, a parallel session ran an experiment nobody had thought to run: render each of NECROPHONE's four engines headlessly, hand the resulting audio straight back to CORONER, and check whether it routes each one home. It is an obvious test in hindsight and it found something on its first run.

One caveat travels with the number. chooseEngine maps material to a suitable engine, not audio to its origin — so a stable harmonic wavetable render genuinely does suit The Revenant, and "2 of 8" is one measurement nobody had taken rather than six bugs. But two of the results are wrong under any reading.

My R² guard was powerless by construction. It refuses poor fits — and this fit was superb. I had guarded the shape and forgotten to guard the magnitude. The declared maximum for the feature was 0.01, five times stiffer than any real string, so an impossible answer never looked out of place in its own range.

Fixed in this session: a plausibility ceiling at 2e−3, the declared range corrected, the feature vector bumped to v3, and two checks added that prove the ceiling refuses an impossible body on its value while its fit is still excellent — and still passes a plausible string. Checks 163 → 168.

A goodness of fit only tells you the shape is right. It says nothing about whether the magnitude is possible. Both need guarding, and only one of them was. The general lesson, worth more than the fix

The second wrong result is not CORONER's fault

Bone & Sinew has no dispersion at all. The physical engine has exactly three parameters and the only allpass filters in NECROPHONE's core are in the reverb. It is a delay line with damping — an ideal flexible string, with zero stiffness by construction. CORONER measured it correctly. It measured a model that is physically incomplete in the one way that defines a struck string, and no internal harness could ever have said so, because NECROPHONE's regression only proves it sounds like itself.

Worse, that wrong verdict came back with confidence 0.94 — a confident wrong answer, which is the exact failure mode this codebase's own notes warn about for jitter.

Three faults in the seam itself

The param contract is clean, better than expected. All 19 ids CORONER can emit exist in NECROPHONE's real registry, every clamp matches its declared range exactly, both enum writes are legal, and a live slip round-tripped through NECROPHONE's real hash decoder dropped nothing.

But there are two copies of one rule with no gate between them. CORONER hardcodes NECROPHONE's ranges; NECROPHONE has no idea CORONER exists; and its loader silently drops any renamed id. CORONER's own comment anticipates this and suggests a release check compare them. There is no such check.

And corpseRoot travels without the corpse. The share format cannot carry audio, so NECROPHONE opens on its default built-in source — meaning a root offset measured from Ben's recording lands on a completely different sample. The granular route needs the audio most and gets it least: Dust & Ashes arrives with an engine selected and nothing to grind.

VI · bThe record as a surface

The shared contract between these six programs is prose, and prose is the least-tested surface in the estate. It went stale twice in one day.

AUDIO_INTERCHANGE.md is where the rules that cross project boundaries live. Nothing enforces those rules except the tests, and the tests only fail after someone has already broken something — so the document is load-bearing in a way source files are not. It is also, by construction, the one part of the estate no harness reads.

Updating it for CORONER produced a small, complete demonstration of why that matters.

Two things went stale, and both were mine

A count nobody re-derives. The log entry announcing CORONER said it had 30 features. It has 31. Nothing checked, because nothing reads prose.

A claim pinned to a version. The same entry verified compatibility with CASKET at FEATURE_VERSION 2 and closed with "the two sessions agree". That was true when written and false a few hours later, falsified by my own change — in a document whose own opening section already warns that its numbers go stale within a day.

Both were corrected in place rather than rewritten away. The original claim is still there, with a note recording that it was true when written, what falsified it, and what survived. Erasing it would have removed the only evidence that the failure mode is real — and this estate's convention is that historical measurements stay historical.

A restated number is not a measured one. The estate learned that about parity counts and built a generator; the same lesson had not yet reached its prose. Why the 168 in the dependency table is marked as hand-typed

What the version bump proved about the seam

CORONER's jump to v3 was the first real test of a clause CASKET wrote before it was needed: a different version is reported in warnings, never refused. It was verified by running it rather than by reading it — a live v3 report handed to intake().

The mismatch was detected and warned, all seven reads were present and in range, a valid state came back, and CASKET's harness stayed green. Nothing had to change on either side. That is the argument for specifying a consumer before a producer exists, demonstrated instead of asserted.

And then it was closed properly. The note asked that the next person to move INTAKE_FEATURE_VERSION do it deliberately rather than to silence the warning. Within the hour, the parallel session did exactly that — checking first that none of the seven fields appears in the v1→v2 or v2→v3 change lists and that all seven still declare the same ranges, then bumping to 3 and adding an assertion so the stamp is derived and cannot drift again. Their own comment puts it better than mine did: a version stamp catching up with a verified fact rather than being waved through. Harness 79 → 80.

One gap this opened

tools/counts.js compiles and runs each project's gates and rewrites the figures in the interchange, so those numbers are derived and cannot go stale. It does not know CORONER exists. So the 168 in the dependency table is hand-typed, and will rot exactly the way the estate already documented hand-typed numbers rotting. It is the first thing in group five of the work below.

And then this section went stale while it was being written

Everything above was true when it was drafted. Between drafting and publishing, the parallel session read the note, verified the seam, moved INTAKE_FEATURE_VERSION to 3, and grew its harness from 79 checks to 80. CORONER has since reached v4, so the gap has reopened by one — and the clause caught that too, on the first attempt again. Three separate numbers in this section were wrong within the hour, and the live demonstration of a version mismatch that the section is built around had already been resolved by the time anyone could read about it.

The same session also audited §6 of the Interchange — the list of open questions that touch more than one project — and found three of its five answers were wrong. Not wrong when written. Wrong now, because the work had moved on somewhere else and the list had not. As their entry puts it: a question that has quietly been answered somewhere else is the most expensive kind of stale text in a document, because it reads as work still to do.

A report on how documentation goes stale, going stale between two paragraphs of itself, is not an embarrassment. It is the strongest evidence the argument has. The only honest way to end this section

Which is why the last item on the work list is to generate this page from the code rather than write it. Every figure here that came from a harness run is a hand-typed number waiting to rot. The estate already learned this once about parity counts and built counts.js in response; the lesson has not yet reached the prose, and this page is currently part of the problem it describes.

Everything in this section was re-checked against the code immediately before publishing. That is the only reason the numbers in it are right, and it is not a method that scales.

VIIIProjection

Four versions, and what each one unlocks.

VIII · bThe first work round

Five tasks, three more confidently-wrong answers closed, and one measurement that was built and then deliberately not used.

The first five items from the work list below are done. The harness went from 173 checks in one file to 300 across six. Two of the five found faults that nothing else had — which is the same pattern as the build itself, and the reason each of them became a permanent gate rather than a one-off run.

The pack carries the body

The routing slip can now emit a pack-1.0 document with the sample inside it. Both remaining seam faults close at once: the corpse root no longer travels without the corpse, and Dust & Ashes no longer arrives with an engine selected and nothing to grind. It also opens in the DAW.

The codec is reproduced rather than imported, so CORONER stays portable — which is only safe because it is gated. The harness lifts NECROPHONE's real encoder and decoder out of the HTML and proves byte-for-byte agreement across sixteen cases, including the asymmetric i16 scale that is the most copied-wrong line in any audio codec. One check asserts that a tidied symmetric scale would disagree, so a future simplification cannot pass quietly.

⚠ The closed loop found two more things on its first run

snap() is not optional, and omitting it fails silently. Setting parameters writes targets; a voice is built from the smoothed current values, and those only advance inside a render. So a note triggered before the first render is built from defaults — whatever you set.

Measured: without it, all four NECROPHONE engines rendered byte-identically. Not approximately. The harness was measuring the default patch four times and labelling the results with four engine names, and every assertion passed.

This matters well beyond one test. Anything that renders NECROPHONE offline must call it — and the training-corpus renderer is the one that counts, where the mistake would poison a million samples and every one of them would look fine.

A correct measurement was being thrown away by a denominator. Pitch confidence was voiced frames over all frames, silence included — which structurally punishes exactly the sounds that decay, because a fast-decaying note is mostly tail.

On a real string render the pitch was found and it was right — 195.7 Hz against a G3 at 195.998 — then fell below the confidence bar, dropped through to the unpitched branch, and came back as "Percussive" at 94%. That is the confidently-wrong verdict this report already flagged, and this was its cause. Silence is not a failure to find a pitch.

A root detector, measured rather than claimed

CORONER now exposes a detector built for NECROPHONE's job, and the harness lifts NECROPHONE's real one and runs both on bodies whose pitch is known by construction.

The stiff-string claim made earlier in this report is now confirmed to the cent: 109.88 Hz against 111.95 on a 110 Hz body — 1.9 cents versus 30.4.

A chord does not make a pitch detector fail. It makes one succeed, at a frequency that is not there. The phantom subharmonic

Handed three notes at once, CORONER returned 53.9 Hz with full confidence, and the incumbent returned 55.1 — a period four times too long fits all three at once. Counting extra spectral peaks does not catch it: the voice estimate read 1, because once you accept the subharmonic, every one of those notes is a harmonic of it. The tell turned out to be physical and simple — is there any energy at the pitch it claims? Real notes measure 0.26 to 1.00, including one with a deliberately weak fundamental. Phantoms measure 0.0003 and 0.0000.

Nothing in NECROPHONE was changed. This measures a candidate; whether to swap detectors is a separate decision with its own risk, and it is Ben's.

And one measurement that was built, then deliberately not used

Pluck position is recoverable from the comb of missing harmonics, and it now is. It is not written into the PALLBEARER patch, and that refusal is the finding rather than a shortfall.

Swept across positions and partial counts, the error is not a clean function of how much comb is visible. A comb at p shares half its nulls with one at p/2, so when only some nulls fall in range, two positions explain the same evidence and the fit picks whichever noise favours. One position fails at 12 partials; another fails at 32. That is an identifiability problem, not a tuning problem — a bar strict enough to fix one still lets the other through.

So the estimator was made strict rather than correct: it answers 6 of 9, and all 6 answers are within 0.03. Reporting a measurement and acting on one are different bars, and this clears only the first. PALLBEARER's importer cannot report — it returns a complete, plausible patch built from defaults and says nothing — so a value right two times in three would arrive, be accepted, and move the whole character of the sound in silence.

The earlier decision to decline was right. The only thing that changed is that the decline is now backed by a measurement instead of an instinct — which is the whole point of having built it.

One guard rail worth naming. PALLBEARER's own seam document warns that string coupling can self-oscillate, and that the slip is safe today by omission rather than by design — predicting that a stiffness measurement is exactly what would tempt someone to add it. CORONER now measures stiffness and pluck position, which is precisely the pair that makes coupling look like the obvious third. A test now fails the moment anyone adds it. Fix the oscillation first, then emit it, in that order.

Third — make the measurements harder to fool
  1. Put the bodies through a room, a microphone and a preamp Convolve the synthesized bodies with impulse responses of increasing size, then add hiss, gentle saturation and proximity bass, and measure how far each feature drifts at every step. This turns "it has not met a room" from an admission into a quantity. noiseRatio in particular has never met a noise floor it did not put there itself.
  2. Put them through a mix bus — the estate's own Run bodies through RIGOR and CASKET and measure how much the envelope features move. Attack, sustain and decay are what the routing slip reads, and compression rewrites all three. This is a test only this estate can write, because it owns both ends of it.
  3. Give every feature a confidence, not just the verdict inharmFit proved how much a measurement's own reliability is worth — it is the only reason the detune confound is caught at all. Every feature should carry one, and the verdict should weight by them rather than trusting all thirty-three equally.
  4. Handle polyphony honestly The voice estimate is a coarse peak count that says so in its own note. Multi-f0 estimation would let the report say "there are three things here" instead of quietly analysing the loudest and presenting it as the answer.
Fourth — the machinery for a model
  1. Build the corpus renderer Headless batch rendering from NECROPHONE and PALLBEARER: random patches in, audio and parameter vectors out, deterministic and resumable. Two warnings already known and load-bearing — the granular engine is not reproducible (only the spectral one is), and a corpus of single held notes would reproduce at scale the exact blindness that eight real files exposed in an afternoon.
  2. Run that corpus through layer 1 and version the result Every rendered pair through the examination, stored as a dataset with the FEATURE_VERSION stamped into it. That stamp is the entire reason the feature registry exists, and it has already moved twice in one day.
  3. Train a classifier for layer 2, and log every disagreement A shallow net over the normalised vector, trained where labels are free, installed through setVerdictEngine() and compared against the rules on Ben's labelled set. Keep whichever wins; keep the rules as the fallback either way. And record every case where the two disagree — that log is worth more than either classifier, because it is a map of exactly where the problem is hard.
  4. Train the inverse-parameter model, one engine at a time Start with The Revenant — fewest parameters, most predictable spectrum. Audio in, parameters out, scored by rendering the prediction and measuring the distance back. Do not touch the granular engine until the analogue one is convincing, and note that granular's irreproducibility makes it the hardest target in the estate rather than merely the last.
Fifth — make it a real member of the estate
  1. Write the C++ twin and its parity gate Once the feature set stops moving — and it has moved twice today — CORONER earns a twin like every other project here, with a gate proving the two agree bit for bit. The FFT will be the interesting part; LAW 1 and LAW 2 both bite hardest there, and PALLBEARER's warning applies: a parity fixture must be at least as portable as the thing it tests.
  2. Make the numbers derived, then build the release check and wire CI Start with tools/counts.js, which compiles and runs every project's gates and rewrites the figures in the Interchange so they cannot go stale — it does not know CORONER exists, so its 168 is hand-typed and will rot exactly as the estate already documented hand-typed numbers rotting. Then one command proving the embed is current, the docs agree with the code, the declared feature count matches the registry, and every gate still bites — that count was already wrong once in this session, and so was a compatibility claim. Then Linux, macOS and Windows, on a Node version that is not the sandbox's, because the estate has been burned twice by a green that only spoke for one architecture and one runtime. Finally, regenerate this page from the code rather than by hand: a report that can go stale is a report that will.

XIFive dreams

Thinking past the roadmap entirely.
DREAM IThe Resurrection Engine, completed
NECROPHONE's founding promise is "feed it the dead, it will make them sing". Today you feed it a corpse and turn knobs until it sings. The dream is that you feed it a corpse and it already knows — CORONER listens to the sample, the inverse model reads it back into parameters, and the instrument arrives at a sound that is recognisably the thing you gave it, ready to be played in a key it never knew, with a decay it never had. Not a sampler playing back a recording. An instrument that has learned a dead sound and can now perform it.
DREAM IIThe archive that plays itself
Point CORONER at everything you have ever recorded — every rehearsal, every voice memo, every show tape, the whole shelf. It listens to all of it and builds a searchable map of your own sound: every plucked thing, every unstable note, every texture, indexed by what it actually is rather than by filename and date. Then you ask it for something — "that scraping sound from the 2019 tapes" — and it finds it, and hands it to NECROPHONE as a patch. Twenty years of accumulated noise becomes a playable library.
DREAM IIIA machine that scores a room
CORONER already measures a room's fingerprint as an obstacle — reverb smearing attack, raising the noise floor. Invert that. Put a microphone in a theatre and let it listen to the space itself: what the room does to a voice, where it rings, how long it holds. Then it writes you the reverb, the EQ curve and the arrangement that will sit in that room. A show that arrives already tuned to the building it is playing in, before load-in.
DREAM IVThe instrument that listens back
Right now the flow is one direction: audio in, patch out, done. The dream closes the loop. You play something; CORONER hears not just what but how — the jitter, the vibrato, the way your attacks land. And the instrument shifts to meet you: the synth's drift matching your unsteadiness, its envelope matching your touch, PALLBEARER's fingering brain choosing strings the way your hand actually moves. An instrument that has taken your measure and plays like you rather than like a preset.
DREAM VThe honest machine, published
The most unusual thing in this codebase is not the DSP. It is that the code argues with itself in the comments — every wrong turn kept, every threshold explaining what data it rests on and how thin that data is, a measurement that ran backwards recorded rather than quietly fixed. Almost nothing shipped in audio software does this, and it is genuinely useful to anyone else building a classifier. The dream is that CORONER goes out into the world not as a product but as a worked example of how to be honest about a measurement you are not sure of — and that the thing people take from it is the method, not the code.
